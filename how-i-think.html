04 / How I Think

A small set of
working principles.

I collect ideas from psychology, reading, and everyday experience. The useful ones become things to try—not rules I have to defend forever.

01 / Starting

Make the next action smaller.

When a task is too vague to begin, give it a visible first step: open the paper, write one question, or sketch one version.

In practice

“Work on a project” → “Write the question the project should answer.”

02 / Evidence

Keep the claim close to the evidence.

A compelling idea is a starting point. Ask what supports it, where it applies, and what the evidence leaves unresolved. A saved post can supply a question; checking its source is a separate part of the work.

A question to keep

What observation would make me change my mind?

03 / Decisions

Decide what would make it worth continuing.

Before a large commitment, write down what progress would look like and when to reconsider. Past effort alone is not a reason to keep going.

In practice

Define the purpose, a small milestone, and a review point.

04 / Learning

Knowing is different from using.

Not every useful idea needs to become an active habit. Keep a reference library, and choose only a few things to test at a time.

My screenshot-sorting experiment gives this distinction a place to start: identify the idea, keep its context, and decide whether to investigate it or try one small action.

A working distinction

Reference → Principle → Default → Experiment → Review

Always open to revision

The notebook stays unfinished.

These are personal working principles. Their value is in the questions and actions they make possible.
